# 02 · Story: Brief, Scenes, Shot list, on TR130

Second issue under the way of working decided on 2026-11-24 (`../HANDOFF.md`, "Start here"). The operator called
Story and Prep "the most important" parts: how an idea in Notion becomes scenes and shots, where names are made,
and how those names reach Review Room.

- **Project:** TR130 — Teaser Trailer. Look only; it is connected to the real Review Room.
- **The app today:** five steps (Brief, Acts, Scenes, Story beats, Shot list), an empty brief form, the old boxy
  look. Nothing like the agreed look.
- **Audition:** `story.html` in the session scratchpad (`cards/`), beside the Shots audition, sharing its styles
  (`app.css`). Built from TR130's real story plan, the imported Bible and the board.

## What the audition shows (first draft, 2026-11-24, not yet judged)

Three steps instead of five, as proposed in the handoff and liked by the operator: **Brief, Scenes, Shot list**,
side by side in the bar above the page, each with a dot that turns teal when approved and one Approve button at
the right that says what approving locks.

- **Brief:** the logline, taken from the Bible's "Logline" section, with where it came from (the Notion page and the
  import date); what it is (teaser trailer, 2:35, 16:9 anamorphic, baseline TR110 rough cut 18, FOREVER); **how
  every shot is named** (`BLR_TR130_DRP_0010_v001`: show, trailer, scene, shot, take) with the rule that scene codes
  lock when Scenes is approved and shot numbers when the Shot list is, and that Review Room uses the same names; and
  the Bible's ten notes for the trailer, three shown, the rest behind "Show all".
- **Scenes:** acts as plain headings; each scene its code, title, shot count and length, and one line of what
  happens. TR130's scenes have no such line, so the audition carries drafts written from each scene's shots and the
  Bible, marked "Draft" in amber. They stand in for what the Agent would draft; none is approved.
- **Shot list:** every scene's shots in order: still, code, shot line, length, takes, and whether the pick is stored
  in Review Room. A row opens that scene in the Shots audition.
- The left list holds the three steps and, under them, the acts and scenes; clicking a scene jumps to it.
- Measured: the steps and the page share one left edge with the rest of the screen, and the first rows of the left
  list and the bar share one centre line.

## Refined the same evening (operator: "yes, refine it"; then "too black and white", "I couldn't see it said
## Logline")

- Section titles (Logline, What it is, How every shot is named, Notes) are teal, like the act headings; the grey
  ones did not read as titles.
- The Brief opens with a strip of eleven stills, one from the middle of each scene, labelled with its code; a still
  opens that scene in Shots. The colour comes from the footage, not from decoration.
- Each scene in Scenes carries a strip of its shots' stills (ten at most, then "+5").
- A scene's line is edited in place by clicking it; an edited line loses its "Draft" mark.
- Approving Scenes puts a small lock before every scene code, removes the "Draft" marks, and the bar then says
  "Scene codes locked". The Shot list cannot be approved before Scenes; approving it locks every shot code the same
  way. The footer names the next thing to do. Approvals are local to the page and reset on reload.
- **The operator asked why the steps matter.** As built, the steps are where the name is made: Scenes fixes the
  scene part (`DRP`), the Shot list fixes the shot part (`0010`). **Decided: keep the bar** as it is.
- **Naming line:** reads show, episode, scene, shot, take; the trailer code (`TR130`) is labelled Episode, since a
  trailer is treated as an episode of its own (`CONTEXT.md`, "Trailer code").

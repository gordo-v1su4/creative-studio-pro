# FRESH IDEA (ONE SHOT Community Edition)

Built by **CodeOneshot / ONE SHOT** (youtube.com/@CodeoneshotAi · codeoneshot.com). Free to share.

The fix for the most common creative dead end: you ask for "something new" and get the same shot with a different
adjective. A fresh idea keeps the **story beat** and throws away **everything about how it's shown**.

## TRIGGERS → HOW MANY

| The user says | Concepts |
|---|---|
| "fresh idea" / "fresh idea for this shot" / "something different" | **2** |
| "N fresh ones" / "give me N more" | **N** |
| "pitch again" / "none of these" | 2 new ones, different from every concept so far |

## STEP 1 — LOCK THE BEAT (silently)

Write down what must NOT change:
- **Story beat:** what happens and why it matters (e.g. "the hero arrives on the battlefield").
- **Cast and references:** the same characters, the same uploaded images, the same powers and colours.
- **Format:** clip length, aspect ratio, prompt style, language, reference-tag syntax. Whatever format
  the conversation is already using, keep it exactly.
- **Every correction the user already gave** ("no glow", "don't let him look at the camera", "keep it under 15 s"). These carry
  into every fresh idea, word for word.

Everything else is open.

## STEP 2 — BREAK IT ON REAL AXES

Each concept must differ from the last version AND from the others on **at least 3** of these:

1. **Staging:** where the characters are and how they're arranged (above/below, near/far, hidden/exposed).
2. **Camera strategy:** a different base style (locked-off wide · slow push · lateral tracking · low ground-skim ·
   overhead crane · whip-pan reveal · long-lens compression · POV · foreground occlusion · rack focus).
3. **Use of the location:** a different part of the place, or the place as a weapon or obstacle (walls, water,
   sand, debris, height).
4. **Entrance or action:** how the beat starts (from above, from behind, out of the weather, already
   mid-fight, through a wall).
5. **Point of view:** whose eyes we're near: hero, villain, a victim, a bystander, an object.
6. **Time and weather:** night/dawn, storm, fog, rain, dust.
7. **Pacing:** real time vs one slow-motion beat, one long take vs 2–3 cuts, quiet then sudden.
8. **Scale:** an intimate close fight vs an epic wide shot of a small figure in a huge space.

**Not a fresh idea:** a different lens on the same staging, a different colour grade, extra adjectives,
more effects, or the same shot from the other side. If a concept could be described as "the last one
but…", throw it away.

## STEP 3 — WRITE EACH CONCEPT IN FULL

For each concept:
1. **Title line** with a colour marker (cycle 🟥 🟧 🟨 🟩 🟦 🟪 across the conversation) + the clip length +
   the upload order, e.g. `🟩 Out of the Storm — 10 s. Upload: <<<image_1>>> = hero, <<<image_2>>> = desert`
2. **One-line pitch** in plain words: what makes it different.
3. **The full prompt** in a copy box. It's ready to paste, as long and detailed as the original, never a
   summary or a diff. Use the same prompt structure the conversation already uses. If none exists yet,
   use: scene + realism lock, references, characters, space, first frame, timeline with seconds,
   physics, camera, light, sound, locks/negatives.

## STEP 4 — PICK ONE

End with **"My pick: <title>"** plus one sentence on why it best serves the beat.

## QUALITY RULES (all concepts)

- **Live-action, not game look:** real lenses, real materials and wear, no glow or neon unless the
  user asks for it.
- **Physically possible:** "crazy" means stunts (wall runs, vaults, throws), not impossible camera rigs.
- **No hero cam:** no victory freeze, no low-angle poster pose. End mid-motion.
- **Every shot earns its place:** it adds distance, route, physicality or consequence, not just a cooler
  angle.
- **Simpler beats safer:** fewer characters and moves per clip means less morphing. One fighter or one duel at a time.
- **Sizes in cm/m only:** never compare to everyday objects, because "melon-sized" gives you a melon.

## WHEN THE USER PICKS ONE

Carry on from that concept. If they say "love it, but …", change only the "but" and keep every other
word. Remember which concepts were already used, so the next "fresh idea" never brings back an old one.
